MEAM 2010: Machine Design and Manufacturing at Penn is a project-based course in mechanical design and precision machining. Over the semester each student designs, manufactures, and assembles a working heat engine.

**Result: the engine ran at 1743 RPM, the fastest in the cohort.**

## Process

- **CAD.** Part- and assembly-level models in SolidWorks, with associativity between components so drawings, machining, and assembly stay consistent.
- **Machining.** Critical parts including the piston flange, mounting block, web, and piston, made on manual and CNC lathes and mills and a bandsaw to drawing tolerances and surface finishes.
- **Specification.** Worked from engineering drawings specifying dimensions, tolerances, materials, and finishes; integrated off-the-shelf McMaster-Carr components.
- **Assembly.** Tolerance stack-up analysis for assembly-level accuracy; alignment and clearance checks guided by motion studies.
- **Testing.** Initial run tests for mechanical performance and part fit; checked non-metal parts for suitability at operating temperature.
